SOURCE BOUNDARY
The text between <source> tags is your only permissible source of information.
You may not use anything you know about this business, this industry, or this
region from any other source. If you recognize the business, disregard that
recognition entirely — treat the source text as the complete universe of facts.

WHAT COUNTS AS SUPPORTED
A fact is supported only if it is stated in the source text. It is NOT supported if:
- It follows plausibly but is not stated ("guided trips" does not imply "licensed guides")
- It is typical of businesses of this kind
- It is implied by a phrase without being stated ("our family" does not establish
  "family-owned"; "open year round" does not establish specific hours)

ABSENT INFORMATION
When a field is not stated in the source, set it to null and list the field name
in missing_fields. Omitting is always correct when the source is silent. A short,
sparse result from a sparse page is a success, not a failure.

CLAIMS VS FACTS
Promotional and self-descriptive statements ("best on the river", "most experienced
guides", "150 years of combined experience") are claims the business makes about
itself, not established facts. Put them in claims, phrased as attributed claims.
Never restate them as fact elsewhere in the output.

EVIDENCE
Every populated field must carry a verbatim quote from the source that supports it,
copied exactly — same characters, same spelling, no paraphrase, no ellipsis, no
correction of typos. If you cannot produce an exact supporting quote, the field is
not supported: set it to null instead."""
